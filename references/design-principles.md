# Design Principles

Use these principles when establishing or reviewing a website's visual direction.

## Brand before trend

Understand the brand, audience, product, and business objective before selecting a visual style. A trend is useful only when it supports the project.

## Hierarchy before decoration

Make the primary message, supporting information, and next action easy to understand. Decoration should reinforce hierarchy, not compete with it.

## Composition and rhythm

Use grids, alignment, spacing, scale, and contrast to create visual rhythm. Vary section composition where it improves the experience, while maintaining a coherent system.

## Typography

Choose typefaces for character and readability. Define a clear type scale, appropriate line lengths, and responsive behavior. Avoid using oversized type as a substitute for a strong concept.

## Color and surfaces

Use a deliberate palette with accessible contrast. Gradients, shadows, borders, and textures should have a clear role.

## Imagery and assets

Prefer authentic product imagery, useful diagrams, photography, or art direction that fits the brand. Never fabricate customer proof, product screenshots, metrics, or endorsements.

## Content and conversion

Preserve factual accuracy. Structure content around user needs and make calls to action clear without making every section a sales pitch.

## Responsive design

Design for small screens as a distinct experience. Revisit composition, type scale, navigation, image crops, and motion rather than simply stacking desktop elements.

## Review questions

- Can a new visitor understand what this is?
- Is the most important information visually obvious?
- Does the design feel specific to this brand?
- Does each section have a reason to exist?
- Are typography and spacing consistent?
- Are interactions discoverable and accessible?
- Does the mobile experience feel intentionally designed?
- Do motion and scrolling improve the experience?
